POV: You’re Opening JINRO at a Korean Table

From bottle whirlpools to cap-flicking games, discover the playful moves you might spot at a Korean gathering.

Why make a moment out of opening a bottle of soju?

A bottle of JINRO arrives at the table. Someone gives it a gentle swirl, and a small whirlpool appears inside. “How do you do that?” a friend asks. An everyday moment briefly becomes the center of attention.

Soju is one of Korea’s signature drinks, often enjoyed with food. At some gatherings, even opening the bottle becomes a small performance of its own. One person might create a whirlpool, while another watches or simply opens the cap as usual.

Not everyone opens a bottle the same way. Here are a few moves you might encounter at a Korean table, along with a small game that sometimes follows.

First Things First: None of This Is Required

The simplest way is still the usual one: unscrew the cap and pour. Swirling or tapping the bottle is not required and does not improve the taste.

The following sections introduce optional opening styles you may see at some Korean gatherings—not a sequence to follow from beginning to end. If you try a move, hold the bottle securely and make sure you have enough space around you. If it feels slippery or uncomfortable, open it as usual.

Move 1. Create a Small Whirlpool Inside the Bottle

The liquid spins, forming a small whirlpool in the center. It lasts only a moment, but that is enough to catch the eye.

Hold the unopened bottle securely and use a small, controlled wrist motion to make the liquid spin. Keep the movement compact and steady rather than shaking the bottle widely or forcefully.

The appeal is the whirlpool itself, not a change in flavor. Watching someone create one and talking about the wrist movement can turn an ordinary bottle opening into a shared moment.

Move 2. Gently Tap the Bottom

You may also see someone give the bottom of the bottle a light tap with their palm before opening it. Some follow a swirl with this tap; others skip the swirl altogether.

One theory suggests that shaking and tapping soju bottles began as a way to remove loose cork fragments. The exact origin is not clearly documented, but today, tapping is simply an opening habit that varies from person to person.

This is not necessary when opening a modern soju bottle. If you try it, hold the bottle securely and give the base only a gentle tap with your palm.

Move 3. Change Your Hand Positions

Same bottle, different hand positions.

Some people cross their hands to hold the bottle and cap, giving an ordinary twist an unexpected look. The change is in the grip: one hand holds the bottle steady while the other turns the cap.

It can look trickier than it is, which is part of the fun. If crossing your hands feels awkward or makes the bottle harder to hold, stick with your usual grip.

Once It Is Open: The Bottle-Cap Game

Opening the bottle does not necessarily mean the cap’s job is over. At some gatherings, it becomes part of a short game.

Players twist the small metal strip left along the edge of the cap into a long tail, then take turns flicking it with a finger. Each group can choose its own rules: the person who flicks the strip off might answer a question or choose the next dish.

The fun is simple: watching to see whose turn makes the strip come off. That brief suspense can bring a few laughs without any drinking penalties.

Metal edges can be sharp, so handle the cap carefully. Keep the game away from food and glasses. Stop once the strip detaches, and remove the loose metal piece from the table immediately. Anyone who does not want to participate should feel free to pass.

One Bottle, Different Styles

Watching a whirlpool or wondering how someone moved their hands can naturally start a conversation. These gestures are less about following etiquette and more about finding a little enjoyment in an everyday moment.

The next time you join a meal with Korean food and JINRO, take a moment to notice how people open the bottle. Some moves may look familiar; others may be new to you. It is fine to find watching more enjoyable than trying them yourself.

However the bottle is opened, what matters is that everyone feels comfortable enjoying the meal.

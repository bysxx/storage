🎬 Parasite | Jjapaguri (a.k.a. Ram-don)

Parasite introduced viewers around the world to two Korean instant noodles.

Jjapaguri combines Chapagetti, a black bean sauce (jjajang) noodle, with Neoguri, a spicy seafood noodle, cooked and mixed in one pot. It started as a recipe Koreans made at home, but it became an instant legend when the wealthy mother in the film asked for it with Korean Hanwoo sirloin. In the English subtitles it was translated as “ram-don,” a mash-up of ramen and udon, which made it even more famous.

It's simple to make, too. Boil both noodles together, drain all but a little water, then stir in the seasoning powders and toss over the heat. No sirloin? Any beef you have at home works. Sweet, savory jjajang sauce hit by the sharp kick of a spicy seafood broth: the textbook sweet-salty-spicy combo.

🎬 Chef | Cuban Sandwich

A chef quits his restaurant job and crosses America with a single food truck. The truck's signature item? The Cuban sandwich, or Cubano. Fun fact: the film's culinary consultant was Roy Choi, the Korean American chef famous for the Kogi food truck.

As the name suggests, the Cuban sandwich was born from the food culture of Cuban immigrants. Mojo roast pork, marinated in garlic and citrus, is stacked with ham, Swiss cheese, pickles, and mustard; then the outside of the bread is buttered and the sandwich is pressed and grilled. The outside turns paper-thin and crisp, while the melted cheese holds everything together inside. The crunch as your knife cuts through is the true highlight of this sandwich.

At home, a frying pan and a heavy pot pressed on top will do the job.

🎬 Home Alone | Cheese Pizza

The movie we rewatch every holiday season, and the one that makes us crave pizza every single time.

In fact, everything in this movie starts with a cheese pizza. The night before the trip, Kevin's older brother eats the plain cheese pizza Kevin had been waiting for, and chaos follows. Later, home alone, Kevin orders a cheese pizza all for himself.

The cheese pizza here is the classic American “plain cheese”: tomato sauce piled high with mozzarella, the simplest pizza there is. That simplicity lets you focus entirely on the rich, savory cheese and its stretchy pull. When you lift a slice and the cheese stretches without breaking, that's a moment worth filming.

Pair it with → JINRO Green Grape

A pizza where cheese is the star pairs beautifully with bright fruit notes. The fresh sweetness of JINRO Green Grape lightens the richness of the cheese, so your hand naturally reaches for the next slice.

🎬 Kamome Diner | Onigiri

A small diner on a back street in Helsinki. Even when not a single customer walks in, the owner, Sachie, never takes onigiri off the menu. Nothing dramatic happens, yet you're hungry by the end. It's a classic comfort film.

Onigiri are Japanese rice balls. Tuck a filling into warm rice, shape it into a triangle by hand, and wrap it in crisp seaweed (nori). Sachie's onigiri in the film come in just three kinds:

  • Salmon — grilled until savory, then flaked
  • Umeboshi — pickled plum so sour it makes you pucker
  • Katsuobushi — umami-rich bonito flakes tossed in soy sauce

Not fancy, but one bite tells you why it's soul food. Put all three on one plate and taste them side by side.

Pair it with → JINRO Peach

Salty, tangy onigiri go well with a soft, juicy sweetness. JINRO Peach gently rounds out the sourness of the umeboshi and leaves a sweet finish, like the lingering afterglow of a quiet film.

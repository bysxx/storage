Choose Your Color: Spooky Halloween Anju with JINRO

Bloody red chicken feet and little ghost rice balls make an easy Korean Halloween spread. Here is how to make both, and how to pick a JINRO to match.

Halloween is all about the look: the costume, the makeup and the group photo. This year, let your snack table join in.

Anju is the Korean word for food served with drinks. For Halloween, we are going red and white: spicy chicken feet in a deep red glaze and small rice balls shaped like ghosts.

Bloody Chicken Feet: A Spicy Korean Anju in Halloween Red

Spicy chicken feet, or dakbal, is a popular anju in Korea. The chili glaze turns a deep, glossy red, which makes it a natural fit for Halloween.

Never tried chicken feet? Think of it as a chewy, saucy snack you eat with your hands. Keep napkins nearby.

Ingredients

  • 500 g chicken feet
  • 1 cup JINRO soju, for boiling
  • 1 green onion
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp fine chili powder
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • A pinch of pepper

How to make it

  1. Rinse the chicken feet. Boil them in water with JINRO, the green onion and the peppercorns until tender, then drain.
  2. In a bowl, mix the chicken feet with the garlic, chili powder, soy sauce, sesame oil and pepper.
  3. Stir-fry over medium heat. Add a splash of water and cook until the sauce turns thick and glossy.

Korean home cooks often add soju to the boiling water to help remove strong smells. Prefer it milder? Use less chili powder.

Ghost Rice Balls: The Easiest Halloween Snack

These little ghosts take only a few minutes and need no special tools.

Ingredients

  • 1 bowl of warm cooked rice
  • 1/2 tbsp sesame seeds
  • 1/2 tbsp sesame oil
  • A pinch of salt
  • Black sesame seeds, for the eyes

How to make it

  1. Mix the rice with the sesame seeds, sesame oil and salt.
  2. With damp hands, shape small mounds with a wide base and a rounded top, like a ghost under a sheet.
  3. Press two black sesame seeds into each one for the eyes.

Set the ghosts on top of the chicken feet, and your Halloween plate is ready. The plain rice also helps balance the heat of the spicy sauce.

Tips for Your Halloween Table

Boil the chicken feet earlier in the day, then glaze them right before friends arrive. The rice balls can be shaped ahead of time, too.

For the table, a red tablecloth, a few mini pumpkins and some plastic spiders are all you need. For photos, shoot the plate from above so the red sauce and white ghosts stand out.

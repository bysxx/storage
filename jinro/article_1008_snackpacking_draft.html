The best thing you ate on your last trip probably wasn't on the itinerary. Takoyaki at Osaka's Kuromon Market, skewer stalls in Bangkok's Yaowarat, a taco counter right next to Coyoacán Market in Mexico City. We mapped out the must-eat food routes in three cities and matched each one with its perfect JINRO Flavor.

How do you plan a trip these days?

It used to be all about “where should we go?” For many Gen Z travelers, the first question is “what are we eating?” You save one plate you saw on your feed, then build the whole route around that spot. Traveling by chasing snacks and street food? That's called Snackpacking.

There's just one rule. Go where the locals line up. A queue is a better map than any guidebook, and the smell of hot oil beats any landmark.

So we did the homework for you. Osaka, Bangkok, Mexico City. The go-to market and street food routes in each city, plus one bottle of JINRO that's a perfect match for the local snacks.

🇯🇵 Osaka — Kuromon Market, the Fridge of “the Nation's Kitchen”

Osaka has been called “the nation's kitchen” for centuries. And if you had to pick its busiest fridge? Kuromon Market, hands down.

Walk down the arcade and there's smoke rising on both sides. Charcoal-grilled scallops, tuna sashimi sliced right in front of you, wagyu skewers seared with a blowtorch. And the one thing you absolutely cannot skip in Osaka: takoyaki. Crispy outside, basically molten cream inside. Watching the sauce, mayo, and bonito flakes dance in the heat, you'll take a bite right away, even knowing you're about to burn the roof of your mouth.

Snackpacker tip — A lot of Kuromon shops close by late afternoon. Pro move: show up between morning and lunch on an empty stomach and stall-hop, grabbing a little bite at each one.

Sweet-salty sauce, creamy mayo, a juicy bite of scallop. This combo needs something clean and crisp, and that's JINRO fresh. It doesn't drown out the flavors, it just resets your palate so you're ready for the next stall.

🇹🇭 Bangkok — Yaowarat, the Night Market That Wakes Up After Sunset

By day, Yaowarat is a Chinatown lined with gold shops and herbal medicine stores. But the second the sun goes down and the neon lights flick on, the whole street turns into one massive night market.

Why's it so famous? Simple. Yaowarat is home to a cluster of stalls and restaurants recognized by the Michelin Guide.

Tonight's main event: seafood. Seafood skewers sizzling over charcoal, plus goong pao (grilled prawns) — whole river prawns the size of your palm. Don't skip the orange prawn fat in the head. Dip it in spicy-sour Thai seafood sauce and that's Bangkok in one bite.

Snackpacker tip — Yaowarat really gets going in the evening. See a long line? Just get in it. The line is the review.

Pair it with → JINRO Grapefruit

Smoky, flame-grilled seafood plus a spicy, tangy sauce? You need something refreshing. JINRO Grapefruit's zesty citrus notes cut through the heat like a squeeze of lime, and cool you down on a hot, sticky night.

🇲🇽 Mexico City — Coyoacán, Tacos After a Market Stroll

In Mexico City, tacos aren't a “meal” — they're basically a daily routine. Breakfast, lunch, past midnight, doesn't matter.

Coyoacán is a charming neighborhood full of pastel buildings and cobblestone streets. Stroll around, swing by Mercado de Coyoacán, then grab a spot at a taco counter next to the market and fold your tacos one-handed. That's the local route.

First timer? Start with Tacos al Pastor, no question. Marinated pork shaved thin off a vertical spit like a kebab, then a slice of pineapple flicked off the top right onto your tortilla. That's the highlight. Add onion, cilantro, a squeeze of lime, and as much salsa as you want. Stopping at one is harder than it sounds.

Snackpacker tip — Tacos are small, so order just two or three per spot, then move on to the next. That's Snackpacking 101.

Sweet pineapple, zesty lime, spicy salsa. A taco already this loaded with fruity flavor flows right into JINRO Green Grape. Its fresh sweetness softens the heat, and suddenly you want one more taco.

Bring Back 2016: The Soju Moments We All Remember

2026 is the new 2016. From flower-crown filters to watermelon soju, look back at the mood of that year and three soju drinks you might remember, with easy ways to make them with JINRO today.

Dog-ear filters. Flower crowns. A Mannequin Challenge at every party. If your feed has been full of throwback photos this year, you already know: 2026 is the new 2016.

The trend took off on TikTok in January, when people started posting their old 2016 photos with captions like “we’re back.” It quickly spread to Instagram, and celebrities joined in with throwbacks of their own. Ten years later, the songs, filters and party moments of that year are back on everyone’s feed.

Soju was part of that mood, too. Here is a look back at three soju drinks that showed up at Korean restaurants and pochas around that time, with simple ways to recreate them using JINRO.

Why Everyone Is Posting 2016 Again

Part of the appeal is the photos themselves: harsh flash, heavy filters and no pressure to look perfect. Many people remember 2016 as a time when posting felt casual and fun.

Looking back at that year is less about copying it exactly and more about bringing back the feeling. For some, that means an old playlist. For others, it means a drink they have not had in years.

1. Yogurt Soju: Sweet, Tangy and Fizzy

Milky white, lightly sweet and a little fizzy, yogurt soju was a familiar order at many Korean pochas.

It combines soju with a small Korean-style yogurt drink and lemon-lime soda. The yogurt drink adds a sweet-tart flavor, and the soda keeps it light.

To make one glass, fill a glass with ice and mix equal parts JINRO Chamisul Fresh, yogurt drink and lemon-lime soda. For a fruitier version, try JINRO Peach instead.

2. Watermelon Soju: Served in the Fruit Itself

This one was made for the camera. A watermelon is cut in half, scooped out and filled with a watermelon and soju mix, then served with a ladle so everyone can pour their own glass.

It became a popular order at Korean restaurants, including spots in New York’s Koreatown. Recipes vary from place to place, so think of it as a style rather than one fixed recipe.

To try it at home, blend and strain the scooped-out watermelon, then mix it with JINRO Chamisul Fresh and a splash of lemon-lime soda. Serve it in the shell with ice and a few watermelon chunks, or simply pour it into glasses for a smaller group.

3. Somaek: The Classic That Never Left

Some drinks never needed a comeback. Somaek, a mix of soju and beer, was a familiar sight at Korean barbecue tables in 2016 and still is today.

Add a small amount of JINRO Chamisul Fresh to a glass of beer and stir gently. A ratio of about three parts soju to seven parts beer is often mentioned as a starting point, but every table seems to have its own preference.
